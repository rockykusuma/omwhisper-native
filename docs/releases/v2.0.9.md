Meetings stop losing the first minute, your custom words finally change what gets typed, and
Parakeet stops occasionally throwing away a whole dictation.

## Recording starts the moment a call is detected

Until now, OmWhisper waited for you to answer the "Record this call?" prompt before it started
recording — so everything said while the prompt sat unnoticed in the corner was gone. In
practice that was often the first minute: introductions, the reason for the call, the decision
someone made before you looked up.

**It now starts recording as soon as it detects a call, and keeps that recording only if you
say yes.** Say "Not now", ignore the prompt twice, or hang up before answering, and the audio
is deleted and no meeting is saved. The prompt says so plainly, because the honest version
matters here: capture has already begun by the time you're asked.

If you miss the prompt, OmWhisper asks once more a minute later — and the recording from the
start of the call is still there waiting.

## Ad-hoc calls get real names

A one-to-one call used to be filed as **"Chat"**, or **"Calendar"**, or whatever tab happened to
be open in Teams when the microphone opened. OmWhisper was reading the wrong window, once, at
the wrong moment.

It now looks for the actual call window, keeps looking while the call gets going, and ignores
app furniture like "Chat", "Calls" and "Activity". When there's still nothing useful — a browser
call, an app that gives nothing away — the meeting is named from its own summary, so you get
something like *"Office onboarding and seating"* instead of the app's name.

**Old recordings can be renamed too**: open one and press **Regenerate summary**.

## Custom vocabulary now actually changes the text

This is the big one, and it was quietly broken.

Adding a word to the **Vocabulary** tab did almost nothing on the default engine. The engine
ignores the word list — measured, not guessed — and the setting that fixes the transcript
afterwards was switched **off** by default. So you could add `appcast`, dictate, and get "app
cast" every single time.

Two changes:

- **Correction is now on by default.** Nothing happens until you add a word, and if you'd
  turned it off deliberately, it stays off.
- **Split words are put back together.** Engines love to break a term in half — "app cast",
  "whisper kit", "Swift UI". Those now snap back to the term you typed.

Measured across every engine, with the words you'd actually add: Apple Speech went from 8.3% to
5.4% word error rate, Parakeet from 8.3% to 2.4%.

## Parakeet could throw away a whole dictation

With a custom vocabulary saved, the Parakeet engine sometimes returned **nothing at all** for a
longer dictation — a ten- or twenty-second stretch of speech, gone, silently. Sometimes it lost
most of it rather than all.

The cause was the engine's word-list feature, which measurably never improved anything on
Parakeet and occasionally destroyed the result. It's gone. Your custom words still work there,
through the correction described above — which is what was doing the useful work all along.

Parakeet also stops downloading a second model it no longer needs.

## Reply Assist tells you what's happening

Double-tapping right ⌥ used to be silent until text appeared — or silent forever, if something
went wrong. Now:

- The overlay shows **DRAFTING** the instant you ask, so you know the request landed.
- Failures say what failed: **NO TEXT FIELD**, **NO AI BACKEND**, **DRAFT FAILED**,
  **FOCUS CHANGED**.
- If there's no conversation to reply to — a terminal, a code editor, a document — it says
  **NOTHING TO REPLY TO** instead of inventing a reply out of nothing. It used to invent one,
  confidently, in your own writing style.

Drafts also read the conversation itself now, rather than the window's surrounding furniture.

## Smaller things

- **Copy Debug Info** no longer freezes the app while it gathers, and shows progress.
- Recordings left behind by a crash are cleaned up at launch instead of accumulating unseen.
- An error in the overlay could be invisible when no dictation was running. It shows now.
